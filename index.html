How It Works

The Key Finding

"Clutch" — doing better than your own normal level under pressure — is not a repeatable skill. The split-half correlation is -0.04 (batting) and 0.03 (bowling). But quality that holds up under pressure IS real: the final rating has reliability of 0.32 (batting) and 0.20 (bowling). Some players genuinely maintain their standards when it matters. Others don't.

1 Win Probability

Monotone HistGradientBoosting trained out-of-fold (5-fold by match). Chase model AUC 0.896. First-innings uses a projected-total model with normal error. Every ball gets a win probability before and after.

2 Pressure Index

PI = Leverage Index × Momentum × Stakes. LI measures how much the win probability could swing. Momentum amplifies for recent wickets or dot balls. Knockout matches get a 1.2× boost. Top 25% are "high pressure" (PI ≥ 1.395).

3 Expected Outcomes

Machine learning models predict what an average player would do on each delivery given the exact situation. Runs Above Expected (RAE) = what you did minus what average would have done, adjusted for wicket value.

4 Fair Comparisons

Opposition quality weighted (full members 1.0, associates 0.60). Empirical-Bayes shrinkage prevents hot streaks from beating sustained excellence. Need ≥150 high-pressure balls and ≥20 matches to qualify.

5 Composite Rating

50% pressure-weighted RAE + 25% context-neutral WPA + 15% consistency + 10% clutch delta. Weights reflect each component's reliability. All z-scored among eligible players.

6 Robustness

300 match-level bootstrap resamples give confidence intervals. Split-half reliability confirms which signals are real. 15 sensitivity scenarios test alternative assumptions.
